Selected engineering work

Forensic reverse engineering

Recovering an undisclosed proprietary level

A discretionary trader published charts marked with a level he would not explain. I reconstructed the full derivation: a volume-anomaly detector isolating the heaviest institutional candle of the Tokyo session, then the delta-weighted point of control inside that candle's footprint.

Initial attempts missed by $20–30. The gap closed to under $1 once I identified that the calculation required one-second sub-bar resolution rather than one-minute — a distinction that changes the volume distribution inside each bar from smeared to effectively tick-exact.

Why it mattersPorted to both Pine Script and a Python replica, then validated by showing the derived level was reproducible at any data resolution — which removed the paid-data dependency entirely.
Validation integrity

Killing a +558% backtest

A laddered limit-order strategy returned +558% over three years in simulation. The live implementation lost money immediately. Rather than tune the live version, I rebuilt the fill engine with correct limit-order mechanics — orders pre-placed and walked forward, never filled on the bar that generated the signal.

The same configuration returned −91%. Every one of six parameter variants lost money. The entire edge had been a single incorrect fill assumption.

Why it mattersThis is the failure mode that destroys retail algorithmic trading, and most developers ship it without knowing. I now run fill-realism as a standing check before any strategy is taken seriously.
Quantitative modelling

Pricing a prop-firm challenge as an expected-value problem

Built a complete rule engine for a funding firm's evaluation programme — daily and static drawdown limits, minimum profitable days, phase progression, payout schedules — then wrapped it in a lifecycle Monte Carlo that started a fresh simulated account on every Monday in the dataset.

6,300 simulated challenges across 33 configurations. The result was decisively negative expected value, and the project was closed before a single fee was paid.

Why it mattersThe deliverable was a defensible "no". Knowing when not to deploy capital is the same engineering discipline as knowing when to.
ML rigour

Catching lookahead inside my own pipeline

A classifier over engineered fib-zone features produced a best-leaf win probability of 0.816 — a result good enough to be suspicious. Ablating a single feature that encoded one bar of forward information collapsed it to 0.333.

The pipeline now ships with an explicit --exclude-lookahead mode, and all reported figures are generated with it on.

Why it mattersSubtle lookahead is the most common silent defect in ML-for-trading. The discipline is building the switch that lets you prove its absence.
Automation

Headless broker-terminal test automation

MT5's strategy tester has no scripting API. I drove the terminal directly instead — compiling expert advisors through MetaEditor's CLI and running batch backtests through generated configuration files, with no GUI, on macOS under Wine.

A ten-run matrix across six strategies, three symbols and five timeframes now executes in under twenty minutes and parses itself into a single comparison table. The equivalent manual process is days of clicking.

Why it mattersMeasurement throughput is the real constraint on strategy development. Making evaluation cheap is what makes honest evaluation possible.

How I work

01

Costs before signals

Spread by hour of day, slippage, swap and commission are modelled before a strategy is judged. An edge that only exists at zero cost is not an edge.

02

Pre-registered kill criteria

Thresholds that would end a project are written down before the work starts, so results are not retrofitted to a conclusion.

03

Out-of-sample or it didn't happen

In-sample numbers are a hypothesis. Every claim gets a holdout, a walk-forward, or a confidence interval that excludes zero.

04

Specification first

Every system ships with a written design document and plan. You get the reasoning, not just the binary.
